## Timely topic ideas
- **"The government is deleting your phone number."** The caller says someone abroad registered a phone in your name. They "transfer" you to a fake police officer with a fake badge number, then call again later with an arrest threat and ask for a "security deposit." FTC rules for viewers: the FTC never asks for money, never transfers you to someone who wants money, never threatens to cut off your number, and never asks you to keep it secret ([Popular Science, Oct 22](https://www.popsci.com/technology/ftc-phone-number-scam/), citing the FTC consumer alert). Suggested one-liner: *"No government agency will cut off your phone or ask you to pay to keep it."*
- **What the FTC's 2026 report says to prioritize.** These figures are from the [FTC report](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2026-older-adults-report-10192026.pdf):
  - Investment scams were the biggest loss category, up 42%, and they often start on social media.
  - Losses to text scams rose 61%.
  - Government impostors came second, and bank impostors third. Both back up the fif08 and "courier card pickup" ideas.
  - Median loss for people 80+ was $2,000, up 21%.
  - Phone-call scams had the highest median loss ($3,000).

  Two gaps in the series stand out: **"Is this investment tip real?"** (social media, crypto) and **"Scam texts."** Both are good future Fix It Friday or Safe & Sound topics. I haven't drafted them.
